ECN Pro — Map User Guide

Everything you need to get the most from the site intelligence map

🗺️ Getting Started

The ECN Pro map is a site intelligence tool for the climbing industry. It combines gym location data with population, demographic, and deprivation datasets to help you evaluate markets, assess sites, and understand the competitive landscape.

Pan and zoom the map using your mouse or trackpad. On mobile, use touch gestures. The map starts centred on the UK and Ireland — all 300+ climbing gyms are shown as orange dots.

💡 Zoom in to see gyms in more detail. Click any gym marker to open its profile panel.

🏟️ Gym Profiles

Click any orange marker on the map to open that gym's profile on the right-hand panel. Each profile shows the gym's name, address, entry price, website, phone number, and amenities.

The Public Transport Nearby section shows rail, tram and bus stops within 600m of the gym, pulled live from OpenStreetMap.

The 20-min Travel Zone buttons draw an isochrone — the area reachable within 20 minutes on foot, by cycle, or by car. Once a zone is drawn, the panel shows the estimated population inside it, the proportion aged 7–40 (the core climbing demographic), and the number of existing gyms within that catchment.

💡 Enable the Population Density layer before drawing a travel zone for the most accurate catchment figures.

📍 Location Analysis

Click anywhere on the map — not on a gym marker — to run a location analysis on that point. A panel appears on the right showing all three travel zones simultaneously (walk, cycle, drive), each with estimated population and gym count.

This is designed for prospective site evaluation: drop a pin on any location to instantly understand how many people can reach it and how many gyms already serve that catchment.

Transport stops within 600m of the clicked point are also fetched and shown on the map as emoji markers.

💡 The three coloured zone outlines (green = walk, blue = cycle, orange = drive) remain visible until you click the ✕ on the panel or select a gym.

🔍 Data Layers

Use the Layers button in the header to switch between four analytical overlays. Only one can be active at a time.

🏗️ Building Types Colours buildings by use — residential, commercial, industrial, leisure. Useful for understanding what surrounds a potential site.
📊 Deprivation Index Shows relative deprivation at small-area level across England, Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland and Ireland. Red = most deprived, green = least deprived.
🧗 Youth Population Shows the proportion of the population aged 7–40 — the primary climbing demographic. Deep purple = high youth share, light yellow = low.
👥 Population Density Raw population per km². Near-white = sparse rural, deep navy = dense urban core. Required for catchment population figures.
💡 Data sources: ONS Census 2021 (England & Wales), NRS SAPE mid-2024 (Scotland), NISRA Census 2021 (Northern Ireland), CSO Census 2022 (Ireland).

📊 Gym Comparison

You can compare travel zones for up to three gyms simultaneously. Open a gym profile, then click 📍 Add to comparison. A coloured slot is assigned to that gym in the comparison tray at the bottom left. Draw a travel zone for each pinned gym using the walk/cycle/drive buttons.

The three zone colours (orange, teal, purple) correspond to Gym 1, 2 and 3 respectively. Use ✕ Clear selection to reset the comparison.

🔎 Search & Filter

Use the search bar in the header to find a gym by name or location. Results appear as you type — click any result to fly to that gym and open its profile.

The filter bar below the header lets you narrow the map to gyms with specific features: bouldering, ropes, café, Moonboard/Kilter Board, kids zone, or speed wall. Select a filter to show only matching gyms.

✏️ Suggest a Correction

If you notice outdated or incorrect information in a gym's profile, open the gym panel and click the Update Info tab. You can submit corrections for the gym's name, address, website, entry price, or report it as permanently closed.

All submissions go to the ECN team for review before any changes are made to the map.
